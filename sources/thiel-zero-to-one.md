# Zero to One: Notes on Startups, or How to Build the Future (Peter Thiel with Blake Masters, 2014)

**Covers:** competition vs monopoly, 0 to 1 vs 1 to n, secrets, the 7 questions, distribution, last mover advantage. **Use for:** judging whether an idea can earn durable profit or will be competed down, whether a first market is small enough to dominate, and the deeper review that follows a quick CENTS pass (see DEM).
**Provenance:** a brain note built from a web research summary of the book (2026-05-29), not page-level reading notes; no page references; synced 2026-10-09.

## Laws

### THI-1 Competition is for losers 🟠
- **Rule:** When an idea enters a market where several players already sell an interchangeable product, judge its profit potential as low, because competition drives profit toward zero. The goal is to make competition irrelevant, not to beat rivals.
- **Violation tells:** the pitch defines the product against named rivals ("like X, but cheaper"); price is the main lever; a buyer could switch to an existing tool tomorrow and lose nothing; the plan assumes winning a price war.
- **Cheapest check:** list every alternative the target buyer uses today, including a general-purpose tool and doing nothing. If several do the same job at a similar price, the idea sits in competition.
- **Source:** Zero to One, central thesis (the author builds on the classical economics result that competition pushes profit toward zero).

### THI-2 Monopoly 🟠
- **Rule:** When judging defensibility, ask whether anyone else can do what this business does, and whether that position will still hold in 10 years. Back ideas where the answer is "nobody, and it will last", because only a monopoly position (in the sense of "nobody else can do this", not an illegal one) keeps high profit over time.
- **Violation tells:** a competent team could copy the offer in a few months; no proprietary asset (technology, data, network, brand, exclusive relationship); the differentiator is a feature a larger player can ship in one release.
- **Cheapest check:** write down what a competent team would need to copy the offer and how long it would take. "A few months and no special access" means Monopoly fails.
- **Source:** Zero to One, monopoly thesis and the Durability question (one of the 7 questions).

### THI-3 Start small 🟠
- **Rule:** When choosing a first market, take a large share of a small, well-defined market before expanding, because dominating a niche is defensible while a small share of a big market is pure competition.
- **Violation tells:** the first-year target is "all small businesses" or a whole country; the plan counts on 1% of a huge market; no segment exists where the product would be the clear number one.
- **Cheapest check:** name the first segment in one line and list its buyers by name. If you cannot, the segment is still too broad to dominate.
- **Source:** Zero to One, the Monopoly question (a big share of a small market first, expansion after).

### THI-4 The 7 questions 🟠
- **Rule:** When an idea passes a quick filter such as CENTS, answer the 7 questions before committing, and treat several weak answers as a stop, because each weak answer is a known way for a startup to fail:
  1. **Engineering:** a technical breakthrough, or a repackaging of existing components?
  2. **Timing:** is now the right moment? Late if a dominant player has just shipped the same feature; early if the market does not exist yet.
  3. **Monopoly:** a big share of a small market? (THI-3)
  4. **People:** the right team? Co-founders aligned on values and complementary in skills.
  5. **Distribution:** a way to both deliver and sell the product? (THI-5)
  6. **Durability:** still defensible in 10 years? (THI-2)
  7. **Secret:** an opportunity others miss? (THI-6)
- **Violation tells:** answers written as hopes ("we will find a channel"); Engineering answered with a wrapper around someone else's technology; Timing ignored while a major platform ships the same feature; Secret answered with a consensus belief.
- **Cheapest check:** write one line of evidence per question, then count the weak lines (see Thresholds for the cut-off).
- **Source:** Zero to One, the 7 questions. Mapping to CENTS from the brain note: Monopoly + Durability judge what CENTS calls Entry; Monopoly + Distribution judge Scale; Control, Need and Time have no direct equivalent here.

### THI-5 Distribution 🟠
- **Rule:** When a plan has no named channel that both reaches buyers and sells to them, judge it incomplete whatever the product quality, because "build it and they will come" is false.
- **Violation tells:** "launch and see"; marketing planned for after the build; a site of a couple of pages counted as an SEO channel; zero conversations with buyers before launch.
- **Cheapest check:** run the intended channel by hand for one week before building more, and count the buyer conversations it starts.
- **Source:** Zero to One, the Distribution question.

### THI-6 The secret question 🟠
- **Rule:** When evaluating an idea, ask "What important truth do very few people agree with you on?" and keep only answers that are both unpopular and true, because that is where uncrowded opportunities hide:
  - consensus answer ("people want X"): not an opportunity;
  - popular and true: fierce competition, weak monopoly;
  - unpopular and false: a delusion, drop it;
  - unpopular and true: a possible secret.
- **Violation tells:** the "insight" is what every player in the market already says; or almost nobody agrees because the evidence contradicts it.
- **Cheapest check:** write the secret in one sentence, then find one piece of evidence that it is true (behavior, payments, data) and one that few act on it (nobody serves it well). Both are required.
- **Source:** Zero to One, the contrarian question the author asks founders in interviews.

### THI-7 0 to 1 vs 1 to n 🟠
- **Rule:** When an idea copies and adapts what exists (one more restaurant, one more SaaS of a known type, a clone), classify it as 1 to n (horizontal progress) and expect capped, competed profit; expect durable high profit only from 0 to 1 ideas (vertical progress) that create what did not exist, because copies compete while new creation can be defended.
- **Violation tells:** the one-line description is "X for Y" where X already exists; the only differences are region, language or price.
- **Cheapest check:** describe the product without naming any existing product. If that is impossible, it is 1 to n.
- **Source:** Zero to One, horizontal progress (copying, globalization, scale) vs vertical progress (creation, technology).

### THI-8 Last mover advantage 🟠
- **Rule:** When an idea's edge is "we are first", give that edge no credit, because the first mover spends to open a market that a later, better entrant captures. Aim to be the last mover: the one that arrives, is clearly better on the key dimensions, and dominates for the long run.
- **Violation tells:** "the first X tool in [language or region]" offered as the moat; no plan to lock a niche after launch; speed to market treated as defensibility.
- **Source:** Zero to One, last mover advantage (the brain note's examples: Google was not the first search engine, Facebook not the first social network, the iPhone not the first smartphone).

## Thresholds and numbers
- More than 3 weak answers out of the 7 questions: abandon (🔴, cut-off from the web research summary; no page cited).
- Durability horizon: still defensible in 10 years (🟠, the Durability question).
- A business that can be copied within 6 months dies (🔴, rule of thumb from the web research summary).
- Competition pushes profit toward zero (🟠, the author's thesis, resting on classical economics).

## Anti-patterns
- **"Like X, but cheaper":** positioning by price against a named rival -> walks into the price war THI-1 predicts.
- **1% of a giant market:** sizing success as a sliver of a huge market -> a small share of a big market is all competition and gives no defensible base.
- **First-mover comfort:** "we are first in this language or region" -> the first mover pays to educate the market for a better follower.
- **Build it and they will come:** product first, channel later -> a product without distribution does not sell.
- **Repackaging sold as engineering:** wrapping existing components (for example a general AI model behind a new interface) and calling it a breakthrough -> fails Engineering and Durability, since any team can rebuild it.

## Limits
- **Survivorship bias:** the author's cases (PayPal, Facebook, Tesla) are winners picked after the fact; many dead startups would have ticked the 7 boxes in hindsight.
- **Secrets are retro-narrative:** a secret is easy to name after success; in real time it looks like a bad idea (Webvan, Pets.com and Theranos each claimed one).
- **Monopoly takes capital:** the path described assumes elite universities and top venture funds; a bootstrapped solo founder cannot follow the same trajectory.
- **"Solo founder = handicap" is contested:** it reflects a venture-capital, scale-or-die view; many indie makers succeed alone.
- **Definite optimism:** the call for detailed long-range plans clashes with how often companies pivot; heavy long-range planning breeds inertia.
- **The author's politics** draw criticism; the brain note says to judge the framework apart from them.
- **Provenance:** the brain note is a web research summary with no page references; specific wordings are the summary's reading of the book.
