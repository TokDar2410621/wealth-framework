# The Laws of Human Nature (Robert Greene, 2018)

**Covers:** emotional buying, buyer desire over builder desire, character read from behavior, shiny-object drift, self-sabotage through planning, defensiveness in selling. **Use for:** judging whether an idea is sold on emotion or on logic, whether partners and buyers are judged on their record, and whether the founder's own patterns (building instead of selling, plan churn, status-seeking) are steering the decision.
**Provenance:** web research summary of the book; the brain note applied 8 of the 18 laws to observed founder failure patterns, rewritten here as generic tells; synced 2026-10-09.

## Laws

### GHN-1 Law 5: The Law of Covetousness 🟠
- **Rule:** When deciding what to build and how to sell it, start from what the buyer wants (peace of mind, visibility, control) instead of what the founder is excited to build, because buyers pay for their own desire and never for the builder's enthusiasm about the stack.
- **Violation tells:** building instead of asking the market; the pitch lists technologies and features; the founder can describe the architecture but cannot state the buyer's outcome in the buyer's words.
- **Cheapest check:** ask five target buyers what they want to stop worrying about, and write the offer in their words before writing code.
- **Source:** The Laws of Human Nature, Law 5, as read by the brain note. The chapter itself centers on desire for what is absent or out of reach; the "focus on what others want" reading is the note's application.

### GHN-2 Law 1: The Law of Irrationality 🟠
- **Rule:** When writing a pitch or an offer, lead with an emotional driver (fear, pride, belonging, desire for control) and use the rational case as support, because people decide on emotion and rationalize afterward; logic completes emotion, it does not replace it.
- **Violation tells:** a pitch built only on diagnostic data and measurable ROI; no fear or desire named; low conversion despite a strong logical case.
- **Cheapest check:** send two openings to the same audience, one naming the fear the buyer lives with and one stating the ROI, and compare replies.
- **Source:** The Laws of Human Nature, Law 1.

### GHN-3 Law 4: The Law of Compulsive Behavior 🟠
- **Rule:** When judging a co-founder, a buyer or yourself, trust repeated behavior over stated intention, because character shows in the patterns people repeat, not in what they declare.
- **Violation tells:** a partner chosen on the pitch instead of on an execution record; buyer "interest" counted as demand with no history of paying for something similar; every commercial problem answered with more code or more documentation.
- **Cheapest check:** ask for the record (what this partner shipped, what this buyer already paid for); for yourself, log one week of actions and count commercial versus technical ones.
- **Source:** The Laws of Human Nature, Law 4.

### GHN-4 Law 6: The Law of Shortsightedness 🟠
- **Rule:** When a new idea feels urgent, reread what was decided before adopting it, because whatever sits in front of us looks like the priority.
- **Violation tells:** a plan declared one day and contradicted the next; a new framework, tool or side project every few days; past decisions never reviewed before new work starts.
- **Cheapest check:** a fixed weekly review that rereads the previous decisions before anything new enters the plan.
- **Source:** The Laws of Human Nature, Law 6.

### GHN-5 Law 8: The Law of Self-sabotage 🟠
- **Rule:** When planning keeps restarting, treat it as a possible way to avoid execution and force one concrete action, because while you plan you cannot fail concretely, and a limiting attitude keeps repeating the same behavior.
- **Violation tells:** a plan written, contradicted, rewritten; procrastination dressed as planning; "not ready yet" with no defined finish line.
- **Cheapest check:** each day, do commercial actions before any technical one (the brain note proposes three), and name the fear behind any delay.
- **Source:** The Laws of Human Nature, Law 8; the note suggests tracing the pattern to its emotional origin (fear of public failure, comfort of endless what-ifs).

### GHN-6 Law 7: The Law of Defensiveness 🟠
- **Rule:** When pitching someone you want to influence, frame the offer as an improvement on their own thinking and confirm their intelligence and free will, because telling people they are wrong triggers defensiveness and ends the conversation.
- **Violation tells:** openings like "you don't understand that..." or "you underestimate..."; cold pitches that correct the prospect; repeated failure to sell to strangers.
- **Cheapest check:** rewrite the opening so it starts from something the prospect already noticed ("you have seen that X changes fast; here is a way to use it"), then compare reply rates with the old version.
- **Source:** The Laws of Human Nature, Law 7.

### GHN-7 Law 2: The Law of Narcissism 🟠
- **Rule:** When choosing a project or a path, ask what share of the decision serves the result and what share serves status or identity, because self-absorption that is denied creates blind spots, while self-absorption that is admitted becomes a data point (healthy narcissism).
- **Violation tells:** identity tied to a title such as "tech founder"; the technically impressive path chosen over the one buyers asked for; discomfort when asked who the project is for.
- **Cheapest check:** write the reason for the decision in one line and label it "result" or "status".
- **Source:** The Laws of Human Nature, Law 2.

### GHN-8 Law 18: The Law of Death Denial 🟠
- **Rule:** When deciding how long to persist with an unproven project, count the time against a finite productive span, because finitude creates urgency and shrinks the fears (rejection, public failure, judgment) that paralyze action.
- **Violation tells:** open-ended timelines; no kill date; buyer conversations avoided for fear of rejection.
- **Cheapest check:** set a deadline and the pass or fail signal the project must hit by then.
- **Source:** The Laws of Human Nature, Law 18.

## Thresholds and numbers
- 12 months on a project that does not work cost roughly 2.5 to 3.3% of a 30 to 40 year productive span (🔴, brain note arithmetic on an assumed span, not from the book).
- Antidote to building instead of selling: three commercial actions before any technical action, every day (🔴, proposed by the brain note, untested).
- Antidote to plan churn: one fixed weekly review of past decisions before adopting new ones (🔴, proposed by the brain note, untested).
- 8 of the 18 laws judged operational for a founder's decisions (🟠, brain note selection).

## Anti-patterns
- **Building before selling:** months of product work before the first buyer conversation -> the product reflects what the builder enjoys, and demand stays unknown (Law 5).
- **Tech answer to a sales problem:** low sales met with more features, code or documentation -> the repeated pattern hides the real gap, which is selling (Law 4).
- **Plan churn:** a new plan each time a new idea appears -> nothing runs long enough to produce a result, and the churn protects the founder from a concrete failure (Laws 6 and 8).
- **Logic-only pitch:** data and ROI with no emotional driver -> buyers agree and do not act (Law 1).
- **Correcting the prospect:** the pitch opens by telling buyers they are wrong -> defensiveness, then silence (Law 7).
- **Declarations over records:** partners and buyers judged on what they say -> stated interest rarely predicts payment or execution (Law 4).

## Limits
- Partly pseudo-scientific: Greene mixes serious psychology (Kahneman, Haidt, Jung, Adam Smith's moral philosophy) with historical anecdotes of uneven quality.
- Over-reduction: behavior explained by a few traits, when culture, context and luck remain large.
- Self-help tone in places, less than in The 48 Laws of Power.
- Not scientifically tested: these are heuristics, not laws in the scientific sense.
- Greene is an essayist, not a psychologist: he synthesizes, he does no primary research.
- The 10 laws not distilled here (Role-playing, Repression, Envy, Grandiosity, Gender Rigidity, Aimlessness, Conformity, Fickleness, Aggression, Generational Myopia) were judged less operational for a founder's decisions.
- More mature and empathic than The 48 Laws of Power (G48): prefer it for psychology, keep G48 for power dynamics and defense.
