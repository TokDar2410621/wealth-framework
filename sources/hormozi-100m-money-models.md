# $100M Money Models: How To Make Money (Alex Hormozi, 2025)

**Covers:** monetization models, offer sequencing, upsells and downsells, continuity and recurring revenue, customer-financed acquisition. **Use for:** judging how a business charges and sequences what it sells, whether growth can self-finance, which monetization link is missing, how to raise lifetime value without new products.
**Provenance:** web research summary (French report, 2026-10-09, index-level verification across convergent secondary sources); synced 2026-10-09. The deep-dive summary page stays in the maintainer's brain.

## Laws

### MNY-1 Client-Financed Acquisition 🟠
- **Rule:** When cash limits growth, sequence offers so one customer generates enough gross profit to pay for acquiring and serving at least two more, because growth then finances itself and ad spend stops being capped by available cash.
- **Violation tells:** acquisition cost repaid over months; paid ads funded from savings; ad spend cut exactly when scaling starts; "we will be profitable once we have volume".
- **Cheapest check:** compare the full cost to acquire one customer with the gross profit collected from that customer in the first 30 days.
- **Source:** $100M Money Models (Client-Financed Acquisition); research summary 2026-10-09.

### MNY-2 The 30-Day Rule 🟠
- **Rule:** When judging a money model, require gross profit within 30 days to cover the full customer acquisition cost, and aim for 30-day profit above 2x CAC, because 30 days matches the interest-free credit card cycle (free working capital) and 2x means the customer pays for itself plus two more.
- **Violation tells:** payback measured in quarters; "lifetime value will save us"; CAC computed without fully loaded costs.
- **Cheapest check:** 30-day gross profit per new customer, divided by fully loaded CAC; below 1 is a no-go for paid acquisition.
- **Source:** $100M Money Models (30-Day Rule, 30-Day 2x CAC Rule); research summary 2026-10-09.

### MNY-3 LTGP:CAC ratio 🟠
- **Rule:** When steering the business, watch lifetime gross profit over CAC, not revenue: 3:1 minimum with no human in the loop, 6:1 with one human, 9:1 with two, 12:1 with three, because humans in the loop add cost and variance that the ratio must absorb.
- **Violation tells:** revenue celebrated while margins bleed; no lifetime gross profit computed per cohort; CAC rising while the ratio is unknown.
- **Cheapest check:** compute LTGP (gross profit, never revenue) per cohort and divide by CAC.
- **Source:** $100M Money Models (LTGP:CAC health ratios, author-stated); research summary 2026-10-09.

### MNY-4 Attraction offers must pay for themselves 🟠
- **Rule:** When building the entry offer, make it profitable on its own from day one and never lose money to "buy" customers, because a loss-making front end turns every new customer into a cash drain and the business cannot afford its own growth.
- **Violation tells:** free trials that bleed cash; entry discounts funded as "marketing"; the front end described as a loss leader.
- **Cheapest check:** unit economics of the entry offer alone, day one, with no future upsell assumed.
- **Source:** $100M Money Models (Attraction Offers); research summary 2026-10-09.

### MNY-5 Upsell the next problem 🟠
- **Rule:** When a customer buys, offer next what solves the new problem the purchase just created ("you cannot have X without Y"), because receptivity peaks right after a yes; present a premium anchor (5 to 10x) first, use a menu with a middle decoy (Economist Play), and credit past purchases toward the higher tier (Rollover).
- **Violation tells:** upsells unrelated to what was just bought; random cross-sells; the premium tier hidden instead of shown first.
- **Cheapest check:** list the three problems that appear immediately after the core purchase; each needs a priced solution.
- **Source:** $100M Money Models (Upsell Offers: Classic, Menu, Anchor, Rollover); research summary 2026-10-09.

### MNY-6 Downsell the terms, never the price 🟠
- **Rule:** When a prospect says no, remember they refused this offer, not all offers: change how they pay (payment plan, trial with penalty) or what they get (feature downsell), never the same thing cheaper, because a downsell is a trade, not a markdown, and discounting trains buyers to wait for the next cut.
- **Violation tells:** "same thing, 20% off if you sign today"; price dropped to close; no cheaper-structured alternative prepared.
- **Cheapest check:** write three downsells that keep price integrity: staggered payment, penalized trial, lighter scope.
- **Source:** $100M Money Models (Downsell Offers); research summary 2026-10-09.

### MNY-7 Continuity last, with a signup prize 🟠
- **Rule:** When adding recurring revenue, build it after attraction and upsells are proven, and sell it with a signup bonus worth more than the first payment, a discount for long commitment, or a waived setup fee (3 to 5x the monthly) against a term commitment, because continuity needs a reason to start now and a reason to stay.
- **Violation tells:** subscriptions launched before the front end converts; continuity offered with no signup incentive; churn accepted as "normal".
- **Cheapest check:** the signup bonus must exceed the first payment in perceived value; bill in 4-week cycles and keep a backup payment method on file.
- **Source:** $100M Money Models (Continuity Offers); research summary 2026-10-09.

### MNY-8 One offer at a time; simple scales 🟠
- **Rule:** When building the model, perfect one offer before adding the next and deploy stages in order (attraction funds the rest), because each stage's cash pays for the next one; prefer 100 ways to sell the same product over 100 products.
- **Violation tells:** the full model launched at once; product sprawl while the core offer is unproven; "fancy" packaging nobody can explain simply.
- **Cheapest check:** name the single offer being perfected this month; everything else waits.
- **Source:** $100M Money Models ("Simple scales, fancy fails"); research summary 2026-10-09.

### MNY-9 Value, volume, velocity 🟠
- **Rule:** When planning growth, multiply three levers: how much each customer pays (value), how many customers (volume), how fast they pay (velocity); doubling all three is 8x, because speed of cash collection matters as much as amount for funding the next round of acquisition.
- **Violation tells:** only ticket size optimized; slow collection ignored; "we will fix cash flow later".
- **Cheapest check:** score the next initiative on which of the three levers it moves and by how much.
- **Source:** $100M Money Models (ascension math); research summary 2026-10-09.

### MNY-10 BAMFAM 🟠
- **Rule:** When any customer interaction ends, book the next one before hanging up, because every touchpoint is an offer opportunity and an unbooked goodbye leaks the relationship.
- **Violation tells:** calls ending with "let me know if you need anything"; no next step dated; follow-up left to the customer's initiative.
- **Cheapest check:** the last five customer interactions: each should have produced a dated next step.
- **Source:** $100M Money Models (BAMFAM: Book A Meeting From A Meeting); research summary 2026-10-09.

## Thresholds and numbers
- 30-day gross profit per customer >= fully loaded CAC (🟠, $100M Money Models); target above 2x CAC (🟠)
- LTGP:CAC 3:1 with no human in the loop, 6:1 with one, 9:1 with two, 12:1 with three (🟠, author-stated ratios)
- Waived setup fee of 3 to 5x the monthly price, waived against a term commitment (🟠, $100M Money Models)
- Bill continuity in 4-week cycles rather than monthly (🟠, $100M Money Models)
- Anchor upsell presented at 5 to 10x the core offer (🟠, $100M Money Models)
- Doubling value, volume and velocity together = 8x growth; tripling each = 27x (🟠, $100M Money Models)

## Anti-patterns
- **Loss-leader front end:** entry offer sold at a loss to "buy" customers -> every new customer deepens the cash hole; growth becomes unaffordable.
- **Discount downsell:** same offer cheaper to close -> trains price drops; a downsell must trade terms or scope, never just price.
- **Continuity first:** subscriptions before a converting front end -> recurring revenue on a leaking bucket.
- **100 products:** new products instead of new ways to sell the proven one -> complexity without cash; simple scales, fancy fails.
- **Revenue vanity:** optimizing revenue while LTGP:CAC decays -> the business looks bigger as it gets weaker.
- **Praying for profit:** CAC above profit, ads cut, personal cash burned -> the death spiral the 30-day rule exists to prevent.

## Limits
- Every case comes from the author's own portfolio and is self-reported, with no independent validation; survivorship bias applies.
- The ratios and the 30-day rule are author-stated rules of thumb, not calibrated studies.
- Launch figures (3.6M copies in 72 hours, Guinness record, $106M launch) are self-declared by the author and publisher.
- No review found in the established business press (Forbes, Inc., Entrepreneur); coverage comes from the author's ecosystem.
- The book assumes an existing business with offers and revenue; pre-revenue founders get less from it.
- "Simple scales, fancy fails" can justify under-investing in genuinely needed complexity.
