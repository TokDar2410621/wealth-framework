# Field laws: execution and distribution (Darius's research sessions, 2026)

**Covers:** focus and portfolio carrying cost, execution bottlenecks and defaults, automation and templates, cold versus warm channel arithmetic, follow-up and attribution. **Use for:** judging whether to launch another product or keep an idle one, where to automate, whether a plan or a piece of advice will actually be carried out, whether a channel is dead or simply untested, how to read a silence, how to fix recurring defects in sales material.
**Provenance:** field sessions: the dated research-and-execution log of one solo founder (field laws 26 to 45), each law with the web research its session summarized, plus a later self-audit grading which laws held in the field; synced 2026-10-09.

## Laws

### FLE-1 Focus dilution: the portfolio of a solo founder without an audience 🟠
- **Rule:** When a solo founder with no audience and no proven distribution engine considers launching another product, judge it as splitting a fixed selling effort across more fronts: below a channel's conversion threshold each front expects about zero deals, so k products x (effort / k) = 0. Freeze new products and put the whole selling effort on the one product closest to paying customers, because building is the low-emotional-cost, near-zero-value way to avoid selling ("productive procrastination"). A portfolio reduces risk only after product-market fit.
- **Violation tells:** several products shipped in the same weeks and zero sales messages sent for any of them; "I shipped" reported as progress; the multi-product success story being copied rests on a large pre-existing audience; one product brings all the revenue while new ones keep starting.
- **Cheapest check:** count the warm sales messages actually sent in the last 14 days, per product. Zero on every front means dilution: freeze, pick one.
- **Source:** Field law 29 (Darius's research sessions); Paddle benchmark (single-product companies grow from $1M to $10M more easily than multi-product ones). Field: a solo founder ran five products in parallel with zero sales outreach on any of them; one product produced all the revenue. The self-audit graded the law "observed on a single case".

### FLE-2 An undistributed portfolio is negative, not zero 🟠
- **Rule:** When an asset (site, app, domain, service) has no paying customer, count it as a negative position: its carrying costs (certificates, keys, credits, quotas, subscriptions, invoices) fall due on their own, while income needs a human act per unit. Give each zero-customer asset a deadline (distributed this week or shut down, no third option) and let a new asset exist only as the replacement of one shut down, because the default drifts toward loss, not toward neutral. Automate the inventory (a weekly email listing each asset, its next due date and its paying customers): a written shut-down rule alone has almost never been applied (FLE-4).
- **Violation tells:** a product called "live" whose URL nobody opened in 30 days; expiry, quota, limit and invoice emails about customer-less assets outnumber payment emails; infrastructure spend near or above recurring revenue while the product count grows; "doing nothing costs nothing".
- **Cheapest check:** search the inbox for the last 14 days for "expired / expiring / quota / limit / invoice" about assets with no paying customer and compare the count with payment receipts. The source pre-registered: 4 or more in 14 days confirms the drift.
- **Source:** Field law 45 (Darius's research sessions); Flexera, State of the Cloud Report 2026 (n = 753, self-reported, the vendor sells cost tools); certificate-outage surveys (Keyfactor/Ponemon 2019, DigiCert 2026). Field: a solo founder's portfolio of several sites with a single paying customer logged about ten carrying-cost incidents in roughly a month (an expired certificate on a product announced as live, exhausted credits, failed builds); infrastructure bills consumed all the recurring revenue. Proven locally on about ten events, 🟠 in general; a second-window test was still pending at sync time.

### FLE-3 Cold-channel arithmetic: read a silence against its expected value 🔴
- **Rule:** When outreach volume is low (single or low double digits) and there is no audience, compute the expected replies before sending and before reading the result: a cold, asynchronous channel at that volume expects about zero deals, so silence is the most likely outcome of a healthy campaign and is no market verdict. Move the next unit of effort to a warm, synchronous contact (a call or direct message to someone who knows you) or commit to volume; do not judge a cold channel before about 46 prospects x 3 to 4 touches on one segment, and do not polish copy for a 4-email send. When a channel is structurally low, switching channel beats rewriting the message.
- **Violation tells:** "cold email doesn't work for us" after 4 to 20 sends; a rewrite after a zero that was the expected outcome; replies judged before receipt plus 2 business days; a measurement zero (no instrument, real revenue hidden) confused with an arithmetic zero (n too small).
- **Cheapest check:** P(0 replies) = (1 - p)^n, with p from a benchmark and n the planned sends. If P(0) is above 10%, the result cannot be a verdict; write down before sending what a zero would exclude.
- **Source:** Field laws 28, 26 and 35 (Darius's research sessions); Belkins (16.5M cold emails); Kooti et al. 2015, WWW (Yahoo Mail, 2M users, 16B emails); Backlinko (12M outreach emails). Field: all of a solo founder's revenue came from warm, synchronous contacts and none from cold or asynchronous ones; a handful of cold emails with no reply matched an expectation of 0.16 to 0.23 replies. The self-audit graded laws 28 and 35 "never tested in the field" (arithmetic and vendor benchmarks only).

### FLE-4 Execution follows the class of the act 🟠
- **Rule:** When predicting whether a planned action or a piece of advice will be carried out, judge its class, not its quality, its repetition or its wording: changes to one's own tools or configuration with the artifact supplied, and serial batches to anonymous recipients, get done; acts aimed at a named person who could answer (warm follow-up, call), deletions, setup inside a third party's system, deployments and commitment devices almost never do. Convert the act into a class that executes before issuing it (a warm follow-up becomes one more line of the cold batch, or a scheduled rule), keep a register of what was applied, and treat day 5 as final.
- **Violation tells:** the same advice issued for the 6th or 20th time with better copy; a plan whose key step is "call X" for someone who has not called X so far; a reformatted request that stays in the same class; no record of which past recommendations were applied.
- **Cheapest check:** take the last 10 recommendations or self-assigned tasks, mark each applied or not by day 5 and tag its class. The split shows in one sitting.
- **Source:** Field laws 44, 30 and 32 (Darius's research sessions); GAO recommendation tracking; Bonaccio & Dalal 2006 (advice taking); Tetlock (scored forecasts); Milkman, Minson & Volpp 2014, Management Science (temptation bundling, 226 participants, effect fades after a context break). Field: a retrospective audit of several dozen recommendations issued to one solo founder found about one in ten applied; every applied one fell in three classes (own tool change, serial cold batch, drafting from supplied copy), none of about a hundred items in the other classes. The same founder answered a warm inbound lead within minutes when the other side wrote first, and fired off a batch of cold emails in minutes while one warm follow-up stalled for days. Retrospective counts, causality unsettled, prospective test pending; this audit refuted and restated the earlier laws 30 (reactive vs initiator) and 32 (reformat rather than re-ask).

### FLE-5 Flip the default, then put the priority in the pipe 🟠
- **Rule:** When an action keeps not happening although content, channel, tool and time are all available, stop asking for willpower or an easier act: make the action happen by inaction (a scheduled, cancellable send with a real cancel window of 12 hours or more). Then route the most valuable item into that same pipe, because a default ships exactly what the pipe contains in the order it contains it, and anything left outside, often the one warm inbound lead, becomes invisible to the only mechanism that works. Never default-send a message that breaks a written promise or states an unverified fact about the recipient.
- **Violation tells:** "just send it" prescribed to someone who has failed to send N times; a ready queue that still waits for a human click (a human gate downstream cancels the default); an automated batch went out while the inbound prospect has no draft in the queue; a valuable lead kept as a to-do instead of a unit of the batch.
- **Cheapest check:** schedule tomorrow's batch to leave by default with a cancel window, count what left, then check whether the highest-value pending lead was in it.
- **Source:** Field laws 38 and 39 (Darius's research sessions); Madrian & Shea 2001 (automatic 401(k) enrollment); Mertens et al. 2022 (choice-architecture meta-analysis, publication bias flagged by the authors); Maier et al. 2022 (about zero after bias correction); Johnson & Goldstein 2003 on organ donation and its 2024-2025 refutations (registrations rose, donations did not, because families decide last). Field: over about two weeks a generator that ran unless cancelled produced a draft every day, while human sending managed none of them; once sending was scheduled, a whole batch left in minutes, but the only inbound prospect, outside the pipe, went more than three weeks without a follow-up. Observed on one case.

### FLE-6 Automating upstream of a manual bottleneck builds stock, not throughput 🟠
- **Rule:** When automating a step that sits upstream of a step that stays manual, expect a longer queue and no extra output: subordinate the generator to the bottleneck (it makes one unit only when one has left, from a capped queue) or automate the bottleneck itself. Treat personalized sales artifacts as perishable: past 72 hours, regenerate instead of sending, because "I looked at your site this week" has become false.
- **Violation tells:** a dashboard counting productions (drafts, pages, edited videos, decks) instead of exits (sends, posts, calls made); a "pending" folder that grew since the automation started while the downstream counter stays flat; queued artifacts with time markers ("this week", "yesterday"); a variable cost (inference, API) that keeps running while nothing leaves.
- **Cheapest check:** compare units produced with units shipped over the last 7 days. If shipped is near zero, cap the queue and stop generation until it empties.
- **Source:** Field law 34 (Darius's research sessions); Goldratt, The Goal / Theory of Constraints (five focusing steps; non-bottlenecks should be deliberately underused); 2026 cold-email benchmarks (Instantly; Saleshandy, 53M emails). Field: a headless prospecting tool produced about ten good drafts in a week and none were sent, with an inference-cost overrun on top. The self-audit graded the law "validated on two or more real events".

### FLE-7 Ship rate is a multiplicative factor; artifact quality is a much weaker one 🔴
- **Rule:** When choosing between improving an outreach artifact and shipping more of it, compute realized value = quality x ship rate x touches x arrival rate: one factor at zero cancels the rest, and quality has the smallest proven amplitude (x1.24 to x1.5 for personalization). An improvement pays only if (quality multiplier) x (ship rate after) > (ship rate before): at x1.24 it must cost less than 19% of the ship rate, 33% at x1.5. Prefer hooks that are true by construction over facts that must be checked before sending; one checkable fact is enough.
- **Violation tells:** template version 3 while 25 drafts wait unsent; a strategy sized on vendor claims of x5 to x15 reply lifts; several verifiable claims about the recipient per message; "AI made us faster" with no measurement.
- **Cheapest check:** send one message that needs no verification at all. If it leaves, review burden was the blocker; if it does not, the block sits upstream of any content and copy work is wasted.
- **Source:** Field law 37 (Darius's research sessions); personalization meta-analysis on e-questionnaires (OR 1.24) and RCT on personalized invitations (RR 1.5); METR 2025 RCT (16 experienced developers, 246 tasks); Snov.io 2026 dataset; vendor claims (Hunter.io, Woodpecker), unaudited. Field: a cold-email generator improved over three versions while its output fell to zero sends in a week with a couple dozen drafts in stock. The self-audit graded the law "never tested in the field", and the founding case's proposed mechanism (review burden) was refuted by its own pre-registered test: a message with nothing to verify, to someone who had written first, did not leave either (see FLE-5).

### FLE-8 Attribute a lead to the channel that triggered it, not the one that served it 🟠
- **Rule:** When crediting a lead or a sale to a channel, credit what made the person think of you and act, not the artifact produced afterwards to help them decide, and expect the error to lean toward the channel that flatters the current thesis. Do not industrialize a channel whose external event counter is zero outside internal tests, because there is nothing to amplify yet, only a launch problem to fund deliberately. The serving artifact keeps its value as a sales tool.
- **Violation tells:** a "product-generated lead" whose first timestamped message came from the prospect; the artifact cited as proof was addressed to the founder; the channel's event log shows only test addresses; attribution settled before anyone asked the customer.
- **Cheapest check:** (1) timestamp of first contact: who spoke first; (2) recipient of the artifact; (3) count of external events in the channel log; (4) ask "Where did you hear about us?" in the follow-up.
- **Source:** Field law 31 (Darius's research sessions). Field: a solo founder credited a lead to a free-audit loop built into the product; timestamps showed the prospect wrote 2.5 hours before the audit ran, the report was addressed to the founder, and over its lifetime the loop had fired 6 times: 5 internal tests and 1 manual run, 0 external events. Observed on one case.

### FLE-9 A reassurance clause can cap a channel's economics 🔴
- **Rule:** Before writing a reassuring promise into a sales message, price what it forbids: "no reply, no follow-up from me" gives up touches 2 to 4, about 60% of a sequence's replies, and once sent it is irrevocable (breaking it costs credibility, keeping it costs replies). Use an announced bounded sequence instead ("If I don't hear back, I'll send one short follow-up next week, then stop.") and keep the bound. In any automated system, store each promise as a field on the contact that mechanically blocks the contrary action.
- **Violation tells:** the clause removes exactly the task the writer was already failing to do (it reassures the sender); an autonomy line ("you are free to ignore this") justified by persuasion research in an asynchronous channel; one automation drafts follow-ups to people another one promised to leave alone; an announced bound then exceeded.
- **Cheapest check:** search every template and draft for no-follow-up promises in all phrasings and languages, and count the contacts already under such a promise.
- **Source:** Field laws 35 and 36 (Darius's research sessions); Fillon, Souchet, Pascual & Girandola 2023, Meta-Psychology (pre-registered meta-analysis of the "But You Are Free" technique); Carpenter 2013, Communication Studies; follow-up benchmarks (OneAway, ModernInbound, Instantly 2026). Field: over a dozen cold emails went out with the clause; days after the fix was ordered, it still sat in every new draft under about ten phrasings in two languages, while another module drafted follow-ups to people just promised none. The cost side rests on vendor benchmarks; the self-audit graded law 35 "never tested in the field".

### FLE-10 Speed is not persistence 🟠
- **Rule:** When an inbound lead arrives, answer within the hour and schedule touches 2 to 6 at the same time, because most of the value sits in the attempts after the first; a fast reply followed by silence loses the lead too. Each follow-up brings something new (the promised audit, a sample, a date) and never blames the silence, because explicit guilt persuades less.
- **Violation tells:** a fast response time, one attempt, then a lost lead; follow-ups saying "you didn't answer my message of [date]" or "I haven't heard back"; no calendar entry for touch 2 when touch 1 goes out.
- **Cheapest check:** for the last 10 inbound leads, log minutes to first reply and number of attempts. A median of one attempt means a persistence problem: the remedy is a scheduled second touch, not faster alerts.
- **Source:** Field laws 31 and 42 (Darius's research sessions); HBR 2011 (2,241 companies); Oldroyd / MIT-InsideSales (15,000+ leads, 100,000+ calls, 2004-2007); O'Keefe 2000 (guilt appeals meta-analysis); Peng et al. 2023, Frontiers in Psychology; Gong (vendor data). Field: a founder answered a warm inbound lead within the hour, made one attempt, and the lead went cold; a blaming line appeared in one of the generated follow-ups. Observed on one case.

### FLE-11 Fix the generator, not the instance 🟠
- **Rule:** When sales material comes from a template, script or prompt, write every fix into that source and verify it by generating a fresh artifact, because a fix made anywhere else (a report, a hand-edited draft) is lost at the next run, and automation multiplies defects as fast as quality. A defect seen twice on the same path belongs to the path: block it with an upstream rule. With an AI generator the defect lives in the meaning, not the words: write a negative semantic constraint ("no promise of X, in any phrasing or language"), check each output automatically (forbidden patterns plus required wording, reject and regenerate), and pass promises made by one module as data to the others.
- **Violation tells:** an audit lists defects and the next generated drafts carry all of them; "replace this sentence" prescribed and the defect returns in new phrasings or another language; one draft edited by hand while the generator makes the next with the same flaw; messages written in a language the recipient's market does not work in, which turns the personalization into proof of a machine.
- **Cheapest check:** change the source, generate one new artifact, audit that one. If the fix is absent, it was applied in the wrong place.
- **Source:** Field laws 33, 36 and 41 (Darius's research sessions); CSA Research, Can't Read, Won't Buy (8,709 consumers, 29 countries); Gong (304,174 emails, call to action by touch); GreatHorn on open-redirect phishing links. Field: defects found in a sent cold email reappeared identically in drafts generated a day later; a corrected clause came back in about ten phrasings across two languages; copied redirect URLs corrupted every link in replies drafted inside existing threads. The self-audit graded all three laws "validated on two or more real events".

### FLE-12 Count the runs of a process, not its units; schedule only a real entry point 🟠
- **Rule:** When a production or sending process delivers in bursts separated by silent days, measure its cadence (business days it ran / business days) rather than its unit count: a process that is not launched is fixed by a scheduled trigger, not by more capacity or better copy. Before scheduling, open the actual mechanism (which script, function or workflow, and what it really does), because a schedule on the wrong entry point runs the wrong path every day; then send at a steady daily rate, because email receivers penalize bursts.
- **Violation tells:** "N units in M days" reported without timestamps; units grouped within seconds with nothing between groups; a cadence prescribed to "the generator" or "the pipeline" with no file, function or workflow named; a large batch sent in two minutes after days of silence.
- **Cheapest check:** list run timestamps over the last 10 business days: ran on 4 or more of 5 days = scheduled; 2 to 3 = launched by hand; 1 or fewer = not launched.
- **Source:** Field laws 42 and 43 (Darius's research sessions); Google, Email sender guidelines ("Avoid sending email in bursts.") and Gmail sending limits. Field: a pipeline ran only a few times in ten days, producing its drafts in seconds whenever it ran; the daily schedule prescribed for it targeted a script that was paused and could not produce follow-ups at all, so leaving that advice unapplied was the right outcome. Observed on one case.

## Thresholds and numbers
- Personalization lifts response by OR 1.24 [1.17-1.32] (e-questionnaire meta-analysis) and RR 1.5 [1.18-1.93] (RCT on invitations); first name in the subject line: +20% opens, +31% leads, -17% unsubscribes (🟢, controlled studies cited in field law 37).
- Vendor claims of personalization lifts (x5 to x15; +52%, +142%) exceed the controlled evidence by a factor of 5 to 10; never size a plan on them (🔴, Hunter.io and Woodpecker claims, field law 37). A less flattering dataset gives 0.58% vs 0.42% reply (🟠, Snov.io 2026).
- A quality gain pays only if it costs less than 19% of the ship rate at x1.24, 33% at x1.5 (arithmetic on 🟢 inputs, field law 37).
- AI-assisted experienced developers were 19% slower while estimating they were 20% faster after the fact (🟢, METR 2025 RCT, n = 16, field law 37).
- Cold email average reply 3.4% to 3.7%; 5.8% under 50 recipients vs 2.1% over 500; 10% to 18% with advanced personalization (🟠, Instantly 2026 and Saleshandy 53M emails, field law 34).
- Cold email to closed deal about 0.215%, about 1 deal per 464 emails; average reply 5.8% (🟠, Belkins 16.5M emails, field law 28).
- Warm reply about 34% vs cold about 5%; LinkedIn InMail 18% to 25%; coordinated multichannel +30% to 50% over email alone (🔴, secondary benchmark summaries, field laws 26 and 28).
- Inbound converts about 14.6% vs warm outreach 5% to 8% vs cold 1.7% (🔴, unnamed commercial benchmarks, field law 30). One inbound lead worth about 10 to 20 cold emails (🔴, the source's own estimate, field law 39).
- Four-touch sequence: marginal replies 1.9%, 1.4%, 1.0%, 0.6%, cumulative 4.9%; touch 1 carries about 40% of replies; 3 to 5 follow-ups give 8.3% vs 4.1% without; complaints pass 0.4% beyond touch 4 (🟠, OneAway, ModernInbound, Whali, field law 35).
- One follow-up adds 65.8% replies; about 42% of replies come from follow-ups (🟠, Backlinko 12M emails, field law 39).
- A no-follow-up clause costs about 60% of replies (4.9% down to 1.9%) and needs 2.6x more prospects to compensate (🔴, arithmetic on vendor inputs, field laws 35 and 36).
- P(0 replies) = (1 - p)^n: 22 prospects x 1 touch at p = 1.9% gives 66%; P(0) under 10% needs p of 9.9% or more at n = 22, or n of 46 or more at p = 4.9% (arithmetic, field law 35).
- A 0/40 result has probability 67% at p = 1%, 30% at 3%, 13% at 5%, 3.6% at 8%, 0.6% at 12%: it excludes exceptional copy, not normal copy (arithmetic, field law 41).
- Judge replies no earlier than receipt plus 2 business days: about 90% of email replies arrive within that window, median about 2 minutes (🟢, Kooti et al. 2015, general email, field law 39).
- Answering an inbound lead within 1 hour makes qualification about 7x more likely; average company response 42 hours, 23% never answer (🟠, HBR 2011, 2,241 companies, field law 31).
- 6 call attempts reach about 90% contact, with most value in attempts 2 to 6 (🟠, Oldroyd / MIT-InsideSales: proven on its own sample; phone, US, 2004-2007, vendor-funded; field law 31).
- Explicit guilt appeals lower persuasion, r = -0.26 (🟢, O'Keefe 2000, 5 studies); guilt appeals overall g = 0.19, weaker when responsibility is pinned on the recipient (🟢 on prosocial and health messages, 🟠 transfer to B2B, Peng et al. 2023); "I never heard back" -14% meetings (🟠, Gong) (field law 42).
- "But You Are Free": g = 0.44 overall but g = 0.11 [-0.18; 0.40], null, in the 7 low-bias studies; stronger face to face (🟢, Fillon et al. 2023, pre-registered, 52 experiments, N = 19,528, field law 36).
- Automatic enrollment +48 points, participation above 85% (🟢, Madrian & Shea 2001); default nudges d = 0.62, publication bias flagged, about zero after correction in Maier et al. 2022 (🟠, Mertens et al. 2022) (field law 38).
- Cancel window of 12 hours or more on any default send (🟠, field law 38).
- A personalized artifact older than 72 hours is regenerated, not sent (🟠, field rule, law 34).
- 7 days of observation before declaring a bottleneck solved; one burst is not a system (🟠, field law 36).
- Cadence: ran on 4 or more of 5 business days = scheduled; 2 to 3 = by hand; 1 or fewer = not launched (🟠, pre-registered thresholds, field law 42).
- Applied recommendations came between 45 minutes and day 4, median about 2 days, none after day 5; base application rates: own-tool change about 0.4, serial cold batch about 0.3, named warm act, deletion, third-party setup, deployment or commitment device about 0.02 (🟠, retrospective audit, field law 44).
- About 70% of recommendations implemented within 4 years (FY2024; 76% to 80% earlier, target 80%), driven by a public register with yearly follow-up (🟢, GAO, field law 44). Management consulting about 50% (🔴, blog survey, n = 39, no protocol). Decision makers move 20% to 30% toward advice (🟢, Bonaccio & Dalal 2006).
- Personal Gmail: 500 messages per day; spam rate under 0.3%, ideally under 0.1%; Postmaster Tools needs your own domain, so a free mailbox's spam rate is unobservable (🟢, Google, field laws 33 and 43). A free mailbox is fine at 6 to 7 sends and risky at 25 to 40 per day (🟠, field law 33).
- 76% prefer to buy in their own language, 40% never buy in another, 65% prefer their language even with poor translation (🟢 for B2C, 🟠 transfer to cold B2B, CSA Research, field law 33).
- First cold touch: interest call to action ("reply yes"); meeting-slot call to action from touch 2 or on inbound (🟠, Gong 304,174 emails, field law 33).
- Second touch: foot-in-the-door effect small, r about .10 to .17 (🟢, Dillard, Hunter & Burgoon 1984; Burger 1999); a returnable gift ("if it's no use, I'll delete it", which must be true) beats plain reciprocity (🟢, Zlatev & Rogers 2020, OBHDP, one field study); a PDF attachment in a cold email costs "20% to 30% deliverability" (🟠, vendors only) (field law 41).
- Wasted cloud spend 29% (🟠, Flexera 2026, self-reported, vendor). Organizations hit by certificate outages: 34% to 81% depending on the survey; the 47-point spread shows none is measured properly, so keep "frequent" and drop the number (🔴, Keyfactor/Ponemon 2019, DigiCert 2026, field law 45).
- Accountability partners raising completion to 65% or 95% ("ASTD"): no primary study, recursive citation, never cite (🔴 myth, field law 39). External deadlines beating self-imposed ones: not replicated (🟠 flagged, Ariely & Wertenbroch 2002 vs Hyndman & Bisin 2026). Written goals shared with a friend, 76% vs 43%: n = 149, self-reported, not replicated (🟠, Matthews 2007).
- Single-product companies grow from $1M to $10M more easily than multi-product ones (🟠, Paddle, field law 29).

## Anti-patterns
- **Productive procrastination:** building product N+1 or one more feature instead of sending the sales message -> building costs little emotionally and returns about zero; selling costs more and returns something (FLE-1).
- **Doing nothing is free:** keeping idle assets online "for later" -> certificates, credits and bills come due on their own, so the default runs toward loss (FLE-2).
- **Polishing a 4-email send:** rewriting copy after a zero at n = 4 -> the zero was the expected outcome; neither copy nor channel was tested (FLE-3).
- **The 20th reminder:** re-issuing the same request in better words -> the class of the act decides, and nothing left undone after day 5 was ever done later (FLE-4).
- **Prescribing the act that already failed N times:** "just send it" -> prescribes the failure; flip the default or change the act's class (FLE-4, FLE-5).
- **Production counter:** reporting drafts, pages and decks produced -> only exits (sent, published, called) change the outcome, and automation inflates the first while hiding the second (FLE-6).
- **Attachment on the first cold touch:** sending the audit as a PDF to a stranger -> spam-filter signals and decision friction; announce the work, deliver it to whoever replies, and do deliver it (field law 34).
- **Promise without delivery:** announcing a deliverable that is not ready to send -> an announced favor left unpaid is worse than none (field law 33).
- **Flattering attribution:** crediting the channel that supports this quarter's thesis -> builds phantom channels and misallocates effort (FLE-8).
- **The self-soothing clause:** "No reply, no follow-up from me" -> gives up most replies for good to calm the sender (FLE-9).
- **Blaming follow-up:** "You didn't answer my message" -> at a few percent reply rate silence is the norm; blame triggers reactance (FLE-10).
- **Search-and-replace on an AI defect:** banning one sentence -> the model paraphrases; only a semantic constraint plus an automatic output check holds (FLE-11).
- **Burst sending:** a large batch in two minutes after days of silence -> receivers penalize bursts, and the process is still not launched (FLE-12).
- **Cadence on a collective noun:** scheduling "the pipeline" without naming the script -> may run the wrong path daily, or nothing (FLE-12).
- **Victory after one burst:** declaring a bottleneck fixed after 48 hours -> a burst is a coincidence until 7 days hold (field law 36).
- **Claiming from the envelope:** judging drafts or files by their metadata or by the first one opened -> drafts listed as empty were fully written, and a batch counted from its first unit flipped the diagnosis (field laws 37 and 42).
- **Sums from memory:** writing a total without recomputing it -> the source logged two wrong sums in seven sessions; a number written from memory is no measurement (field laws 36 and 43).
- **"Not measured" without searching:** declaring a metric unavailable -> a vendor's transactional emails (payment notices, billing alerts) often measure it for free (field law 41).
- **Plan over verdict:** re-issuing a plan that a fresher, dated, evidenced field result contradicts on the same channel, or building an asset that already exists -> relaunches a dead channel and duplicates work; the dated field verdict governs execution (field law 26).

## Limits
- One operator: every field law comes from one solo founder's logs over about three months in 2026, mostly B2B cold email from a free mailbox. The laws are strong on the seller's own behavior and instruments and weak on buyers: the source's self-audit found no buyer-side law validated in 62 execution sessions.
- Fast revision: later laws refuted and restated earlier ones (reactive vs initiator, reformat rather than re-ask), and diagnoses flipped (review burden became default direction; quota starvation became cadence). Later laws override earlier ones; expect more revisions.
- Retrospective counting: the act-class audit counts to plus or minus 2 and cannot rule out that easy-class advice was issued when the founder was already acting; the prospective test was pending at sync time.
- Benchmark inputs: most reply-rate numbers come from vendors with unpublished protocols and a commercial interest. The arithmetic is only as good as p.
- Defaults: the evidence is contested (Maier et al. 2022 finds about zero after bias correction), a self-set default differs from an employer-set one, and the field confirmation is a single batch.
- Behavioral explanations (fear of refusal, emotional cost) are the source's interpretation; the measured part is what was sent, and when.
- Pending at sync time: the second-window carrying-cost count (FLE-2, due 2026-10-15) and the prospective act-class test (FLE-4).
