# The Millionaire Fastlane: Crack the Code to Wealth and Live Rich for a Lifetime (MJ DeMarco, 2011)

**Covers:** the CENTS commandments, Fastlane vs Slowlane, platform dependence, income detached from time, scale (magnitude x reach). **Use for:** a 5-minute pre-validation of any business idea before building, periodic re-scoring of running projects, and choosing which weak commandment to fix first.
**Provenance:** a brain note built from a web research summary of the book and its French edition (*L'Autoroute du Millionnaire*), 2026-05-29, plus one field case; no page references; synced 2026-10-09.

## Laws

### DEM-1 CENTS 🟠
- **Rule:** When judging whether an idea is a real Fastlane business, check all 5 commandments: Control, Entry, Need, Time, Scale. An idea that fails one or two of them is a Slowlane in disguise (a capped solo job), because each failed commandment either caps income or hands it to someone else's decision.
- **Violation tells:** the idea is defended on one strong commandment ("it scales") while another is ignored; the founder cannot say which commandment is weakest.
- **Cheapest check:** score it (DEM-2).
- **Source:** The Millionaire Fastlane, the CENTS framework.

### DEM-2 CENTS score 🔴
- **Rule:** When pre-validating an idea, score each commandment from 1 (catastrophic) to 5 (excellent), total out of 25, then:
  - under 15/25: do not start (rework the idea or drop it);
  - 15-20/25: name the weak commandments and a reinforcement mechanism for each (DEM-3 to DEM-7) before writing code;
  - 20+/25: go, focus on execution.
  Score before writing any code, before running a strategy debate, periodically on running projects (a score that falls over time is an alarm), and when a project stalls (one weak commandment may block everything). Sequence: CENTS first, offer design second (see HOR, Grand Slam Offer), because an irresistible offer cannot rescue an idea under 15.
- **Violation tells:** building started with no score; a score under 15 answered with "we will fix it after launch"; no re-score in months.
- **Cheapest check:** the score takes about 5 minutes. Generic example: a template shop sold only through one third-party marketplace scores Control 2, Entry 2, Need 3, Time 4, Scale 4 = 15/25, so Control and Entry get fixed before more building.
- **Source:** the 5 commandments are the author's; the 1-to-5 scale and the cut-offs reached the brain through a web research summary with no page reference, so the numbers may not be the author's.

### DEM-3 Control 🟠
- **Rule:** When revenue depends on a boss, an algorithm or a third-party platform that can cut it off, score Control low and reinforce it by diversifying revenue sources, because that actor can end the business with one decision.
- **Violation tells:** affiliate income from one marketplace; dropshipping on one store platform; a monetized channel on one video platform; a product whose core runs on one vendor's API or one social network with no fallback.
- **Cheapest check:** list every third party that could switch off revenue tomorrow and the share of revenue each one controls.
- **Source:** CENTS, Control.

### DEM-4 Entry 🟠
- **Rule:** When a competitor could launch the same thing within a week, score Entry low and reinforce it through extreme specialization (a very narrow niche), an exclusive partnership, proprietary data or a strong brand, because an offer that is easy to copy gets commoditized: margins collapse into a race to the lowest price.
- **Violation tells:** no asset a copier would lack; several near-identical tools exist; customers compare on price only.
- **Cheapest check:** estimate how many days a competent operator needs to clone the offer.
- **Source:** CENTS, Entry (THI-2 and THI-4 judge the same ground in more depth).

### DEM-5 Need 🟠
- **Rule:** When the idea relieves a nice-to-have rather than an urgent, active pain, score Need low and reframe toward the person who suffers right now and would pay tomorrow to fix it tonight, because a nice-to-have attracts the curious, not buyers: weak conversion and heavy churn.
- **Violation tells:** the benefit reads "do X better" or "be more productive"; prospects say "interesting" and nobody asks the price; nobody pays for a workaround today.
- **Cheapest check:** map the customer's concrete fears, find people already spending money or hours on a workaround, and ask them for payment before building.
- **Source:** CENTS, Need.

### DEM-6 Time 🟠
- **Rule:** When income stops in the month the founder stops working, score Time low and productize (automation, software, outsourcing, a team), because income tied to hours is a job called freelance, consulting or agency, not a business.
- **Violation tells:** revenue equals hours times rate; every sale needs the founder's labor; a two-sided marketplace that needs permanent manual coordination.
- **Cheapest check:** answer "if I stop for one month, which revenue still arrives?"
- **Source:** CENTS, Time.

### DEM-7 Scale 🟠
- **Rule:** When magnitude (the highest price you can charge) times reach (the volume you can serve) hits a ceiling, score Scale low and raise one factor: magnitude (a much higher price on a premium target) or reach (local to global, consumer to business), because a capped business cannot grow past its geographic, human-capacity or niche-size limit.
- **Violation tells:** a service limited to one town; delivery capacity equals the founder's hours; the total number of buyers in the niche counts in dozens.
- **Cheapest check:** multiply a realistic top price by the number of reachable buyers; if the result is small for the effort, Scale fails.
- **Source:** CENTS, Scale.

### DEM-8 Pre-validate before building 🟠
- **Rule:** When tempted to start building, run the CENTS score first, because the check takes minutes while building an idea that fails it can take a year to reach the same verdict.
- **Violation tells:** months of code before any scoring; strategy debates on an idea that was never scored.
- **Source:** field: a solo founder's SaaS, 12 months of building and $0 revenue, reached the verdict that a 5-minute CENTS score gave in hindsight (observed once).

## Thresholds and numbers
- Each commandment scored 1 to 5, total out of 25 (🔴, scoring grid from a web research summary).
- Under 15/25: do not start; 15-20/25: fix the weak commandments first; 20+/25: go (🔴, same source; the bands share the value 20 as given).
- The CENTS check takes about 5 minutes; skipping it cost 12 months and $0 revenue in one lived case (🟠, field, observed once).
- Weak Entry: a competitor could launch the same thing within a week (🟠, CENTS Entry as summarized).
- Need probe: who would pay about $200 tomorrow to fix the problem tonight? (🔴, rule of thumb in the brain note).
- Magnitude lever: raise the price about 10x on a premium target (🔴, rule of thumb in the brain note).

## Anti-patterns
- **Disguised Slowlane:** freelance, consulting or an agency presented as a business -> income stops when the founder stops (fails Time).
- **Platform sharecropping:** a business that lives on one marketplace, store platform or algorithm -> one policy change ends revenue (fails Control).
- **Vitamin pitch:** "post better", "work smarter" -> attracts the curious, not buyers (fails Need).
- **One-week clone:** an offer anyone can ship in a week -> race to the bottom on price (fails Entry).
- **Debate before filter:** a long strategy debate on an idea that fails CENTS -> the debate is wasted whatever its outcome.
- **Fix it after launch:** building on a score under 15 and promising to repair the weak commandments later -> the weak commandment stays and blocks the project.

## Limits
- **Fanciful statistics:** the brain note rejects the author's 1-in-7 odds of Fastlane success as unfounded; its own counter-figures (80% of startups failing within a year, under 1% reaching $1M) carry no source either. Quote none of them (🔴).
- **Contempt for the Slowlane:** the tone toward people with ordinary jobs is morally doubtful and strategically wrong; many Fastlane builders bootstrapped while employed.
- **Survivorship and era bias:** the author's fortune came from the dot-com era (a limousine-booking website he later sold); many tactics that worked around 1998-2002 fail in today's saturated markets.
- **No financial plan B:** the book ignores that building a Fastlane takes money a job can provide; Dalio's "find your floor before you climb" (see DAL) tempers its all-in stance.
- **Burnout is absent:** the book never mentions solo-founder burnout, which the brain note calls the biggest risk (that ranking is unsourced, 🔴).
- **The grid is a convention:** the 1-to-5 scale and its cut-offs carry no page reference; use them as a working convention, not as the author's numbers.
- **Entry and Scale pull against each other:** narrowing the niche to raise Entry can push Scale down; re-score both after any repositioning.
