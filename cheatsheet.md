# Cheatsheet: decision rules across all sources

Use this first. Each line names the law to open when the case is close. Levels: 🟢 proven,
🟠 claimed or lived once, 🔴 to test. Breaking a 🟢 law weighs more than going against a 🔴 one.

## 1. First pass on any idea (two minutes)

| Ask | Law | Kill signal |
|---|---|---|
| Is there a starving crowd: real pain, money to pay, easy to target, growing? | HOR-1 🟠 | No identifiable buyer in pain |
| Urgent pain, or nice-to-have? | DEM-5 🟠, FLS-1 🟠 | "It would be nice"; buyers are curious, not paying |
| Which ONE channel reaches and sells to buyers? | THI-5 🟠, LDS-4 🟠, G48-2 🟠 | "Build it and they will come" |
| New category (0 to 1) or a copy (1 to n) into interchangeable competition? | THI-7 🟠, THI-1 🟠 | Several players selling the same thing; edge = "we are first" (THI-8) |
| CENTS score out of 25 | DEM-2 🔴 | Under 15: do not start |
| Does the business stay alive at the current burn? | TAB-1 🟠 | Default dead and the bet makes it worse |
| Does the stake touch survival cash? | TAB-2 🟠 | Yes: no-go whatever the upside |
| Reversible or one-way door? | TAB-3 🟠 | Sets the depth of analysis, not the verdict |

## 2. The solo founder's traps

- **Another product while the current ones have no paying customer** -> judge it as splitting a fixed selling effort (FLE-1 🟠); every undistributed asset is a negative position, not zero (FLE-2 🟠). Default verdict: sell what exists first.
- **Long solo stretches, no buyer contact** -> require a counterweight: buyers, peers, someone who disagrees (G48-3 🟠, DAL-6 🟠).
- **The new idea feels urgent** -> reread what was decided before (GHN-4 🟠). Guard: is this a new toy to avoid executing? (DAL-3, GHN-5 🟠).
- **"I'll know if it works once it's built"** -> pre-validate before building; the check takes minutes, the build a year (DEM-8 🟠).

## 3. Offer and price

- **Price from perceived value, never cost plus margin:** Value = (dream outcome x perceived likelihood) / (time delay x effort) (HOR-2 🟠). Fix the weakest term first.
- **Price objection** -> add value (bonus, speed, less effort, stronger guarantee), never discount (HOR-4 🟠). Never position as "cheaper" (FLS-3 🟠).
- **Buyer pays before seeing the result** -> a specific, self-triggering guarantee tied to an observable result (FLS-2 🟠, HOR-5 🟠).
- **Open on the buyer's pain, desire or status, in their words**; the product is the proof (FLS-1 🟠). Test before sending: "you" in the first sentence, a lived result not a metric, understood without effort; one "no" means rewrite (KAW-5 🟠).
- **Bonuses kill one named objection each** (HOR-9 🟠). **Scarcity and urgency only when true** (HOR-10 🟠, FLS-6 🟢). Admit one real minor limit (FLS-6 🟢).
- **Move perception before paying for real improvement**, through a visible lever (HOR-6 🟠, FLS-7 🟠). Frame the outcome through status (HOR-7 🟠).
- **Cash limits growth** -> structure payments so the upfront amount covers acquisition (HOR-8 🟠).
- **Prevention offer** -> threat and credible simple solution in the same sentence (FLS-8 🟢).

## 4. Getting the first customers

- **Name the channel**: warm outreach, free content, cold outreach or paid ads (LDS-4 🟠). Volume: 100 primary actions a day (LDS-5 🟠).
- **Warm before cold.** At low volume with no audience, a cold asynchronous channel expects about zero deals: compute expected replies before reading a silence (FLE-3 🔴).
- **The warm ask that never leaves** is a calibration error: people say yes about three times more often than askers expect (FLS-9 🟢). Ask orally, at the next live contact.
- **Inbound lead** -> answer within the hour AND schedule follow-ups 2 to 6 at once (FLE-10 🟠).
- **0 to about 20 customers** -> do things that don't scale: recruit and set up by hand (TAB-5 🟠).
- **Lead magnet** only when the core offer is too expensive or committing to sell cold (LDS-2 🟠); solve one narrow problem completely (LDS-1 🟠); leads but no sales means the wrong problem, not low traffic (LDS-6 🟠).
- **After a customer lives the value** -> ask for a testimonial and an introduction (FLS-10 🟢).
- **Cold contact in Canada** -> phone a published number first; email or DM only under implied or express consent (FLS-12 🟢). Elsewhere: check the local anti-spam law first.

## 5. Nothing moves: execution diagnostics

- **Prepared but not sent** -> realized value = quality x ship rate x touches x arrival rate; a zero ship rate cancels everything (FLE-7 🔴). Do not improve the artifact; ship it.
- **The same action keeps not happening** -> flip the default: make it happen unless cancelled (FLE-5 🟠); predict by the class of the act, not its quality (FLE-4 🟠); bind it with a date, a witness and a stake (FLS-11 🟠).
- **Automation upstream of a manual step** builds stock, not throughput (FLE-6 🟠).
- **Planning keeps restarting** -> a way to avoid failing concretely: force one concrete action today (GHN-5 🟠).
- **A test failed** -> design or execution? First check the volume was really sent and the plan really followed. Execution failure: fix the volume. Design failure (the volume was there, the market said no): fix the offer, the target or the price. See `sources/dalio-principles.md`, "Design or execution?" (DAL-2, DAL-3 🟠).

## 6. Big or irreversible decisions

- **One-way door** (lease, hire, raise, quitting a job): deliberate; two-way door: decide within hours on about 70% of the information (TAB-3 🟠).
- **Write the probability once**, decide whether the payoff justifies the stake, then stop reopening it weekly (TAB-8 🟠).
- **Never risk survival cash**; keep most of it safe, a small share on unlimited upside (TAB-2 🟠).
- **Outside capital or a partner** -> lock control by structure before the money (TAB-11 🟠).
- **Advice** -> weight it by the adviser's record on this exact problem, not rank or volume (DAL-5 🟠); seek smart disagreement before acting (DAL-6 🟠).
- **Market timing** -> position on what a platform shift is opening (TAB-12 🟠).
- **Life-scale bet** -> regret minimization, bounded by the ruin floor (TAB-10 🔴).

## 7. Pitch and persuasion

- Market size bottom-up from reachable buyers, never an analyst total (KAW-1 🟠).
- Show a checkable result rather than argue (G48-1 🟠); demo instead of slides, close on a dated next step (KAW-7 🟠, FLS-5 🟠).
- Frame the offer as an improvement on the buyer's own thinking (GHN-6 🟠); lead with the emotional driver, logic as support (GHN-2 🟠).
- Say less than you could (G48-7 🟠); state what the other side gains, not what you deserve (G48-5 🟠).

## Thresholds

| Number | Level | Law |
|---|---|---|
| CENTS: under 15 do not start; 15 to 20 fix the weak commandments first; 20+ go | 🔴 | DEM-2 |
| 100 primary lead-generation actions per day | 🟠 | LDS-5 |
| Reversible decision: decide within hours on about 70% of the information | 🟠 | TAB-3 |
| Do things that don't scale until about 20 customers | 🟠 | TAB-5 |
| Askers expect about 7 asks per yes; it takes about 2 | 🟢 | FLS-9 |
| Several weak answers to Thiel's 7 questions: stop | 🔴 | THI-4 |
| Touches 2 to 4 hold about 60% of a sequence's replies | 🔴 | FLE-9 |

## Tells: phrases that should trigger a law

| Heard | Open |
|---|---|
| "We have no competition" / "if we get 1% of the market" / a $50B slide | KAW-1, THI-1 |
| "We're first" | THI-8 |
| "Once it's built, people will come" | THI-5, LDS-4, G48-2 |
| "I'll launch another one, this one doesn't sell yet" | FLE-1, FLE-2 |
| "It's ready, I just haven't sent it" | FLE-7, FLE-5, FLS-9 |
| "I'll make it cheaper than X" | FLS-3, HOR-4 |
| "Let me redo the plan first" | GHN-5, GHN-4 |
| "Friend price?" | G48-5 |
| "Should I quit my job / take this money / sign this lease?" | TAB-3, TAB-2, TAB-11 |
